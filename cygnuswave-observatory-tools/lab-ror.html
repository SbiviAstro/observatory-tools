Building Shape

Most buildings are a simple rectangle — the width/length above cover that. Non-rectangular buildings (angled corners, an extension, an irregular footprint) can be described directly as up to 6 walls, each its own distance and angle from center.

Angle is measured from North (0°), clockwise — same convention as azimuth. Distance is the perpendicular distance from the building's center out to that wall.

Roll-Off Roof

The roof rolls off past one wall and parks there — that parked structure is its own obstruction, not just an open gap.

how far the roof slides past that wall

How far into the roof structure the mount may reach. A standard truss with its bottom chord across the wall top gives zero. A raised tie gives whatever it was raised by; rafters with a ridge beam give the whole rise.

usually close to wall height; taller if on a raised track

Rise:run — 1:6 means one of rise for every six of run. Or give the angle; the two stay in step, so use whichever your plans quote.
:or°
Ridge height: —
wall height + (half-span × rise ÷ run)

The ridge is assumed to run parallel to the roll-off direction — obstruction is higher looking toward the middle of the roof's width and lower looking toward its edges, tapering linearly between ridge and eave.

Local Horizon

Terrain beyond the building — trees, other structures. Layered on top of the wall/roof obstruction; whichever blocks more at a given azimuth wins.

your own limit, not an obstruction — most imaging avoids the thick air below about 15°. Set 0 to apply none, and only the building will block a pointing
°

Load a horizon .hrz file — the same azimuth/altitude format Stellarium's custom horizon files use (comma or space separated, "#" comment lines ignored; .csv/.txt also accepted) — or paste/edit the points directly below.

Telescope points at—Blocked by—Obstruction limit—Margin—
Key
  • Walls — solid up to wall height, the building's actual footprint
  • Parked roof box — the rolled-off roof's obstruction, past whichever wall it slides beyond
  • Usable roof space (blue) — the room above the wall top that the roof structure leaves; it follows the pitch, so it tapers to nothing at the eaves. Zero for a standard truss
  • N / E / S / W — cardinal directions at the walls
  • Small bar — OTA
  • Amber line — optical axis
  • Rust-colored skyline — your local (terrain) horizon limit
  • Blue arc — the celestial equator (Dec=0); genuinely tilts with latitude, crossing the horizon due East and due West
  • Bright dot — the elevated celestial pole, at altitude = your latitude — where a polar-aligned GEM's RA axis points

Reading this

Center = zenith, edge = horizon, up = North, right = East. The outer boundary traces your building's actual wall shape — a 4-wall rectangle shows as a rectangle, a 6-wall polygon shows its true footprint. Updates live as you change your setup.

● Green — clear of walls, roof, and horizon
● Red — blocked by a wall or the parked roof
● Brown — below your local (terrain) horizon
